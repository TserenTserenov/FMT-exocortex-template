# Why Skills Are Not Enough: Principles and the Generative Hierarchy

## Core Idea

Skills are a necessary but **insufficient** level of mastery. They occupy the lowest level of a four-level hierarchy of principles and are **generated by** those principles — they do not replace them.

## Four Levels

| Level | What | Why an agent needs it | Without it |
|-------|------|-----------------------|------------|
| **Level 0: ZP** | Zero principles (transdisciplines) | Ability to navigate any new domain | Every domain change means starting from scratch |
| **Level 1: FPF** | First principles (correctness frame) | Precise language: Role ≠ Method ≠ Work ≠ Capability | Everything is called "a process" — confusion follows |
| **Level 2: Pack** | Second principles (domain knowledge) | Source-of-truth: distinctions, methods, failure modes | Tribal knowledge; every project starts over |
| **Level 3: DS** | Skills, programs, tools | Concrete execution | Nothing to do |

**Fallback chain:** DS → Pack → SPF → FPF → ZP. When something is unclear at the DS level, move up to Pack. If Pack does not help, move to FPF. Continue up to ZP.

## Four Arguments: Why Skills Are Not Enough

### 1. A Skill Is Procedural, Not Strategic

A skill is an automated sequence of operations. It answers the question **"how to perform THIS procedure."** The question **"WHICH procedure to choose"** is knowledge generated by principles.

Analogy from ML (MIM.D.007): a principle is the inductive bias of an architecture; a skill is the trained weights. Without the right bias, no amount of data helps.

### 2. Skills Do Not Transfer Across Domains

A programmer's skill is useless in surgery. An accountant's skill is useless in design. Disciplinary skills are bound to their context.

Zero principles (ZP) and transdisciplines transfer by definition: ontology, logic, and Systems Thinking work everywhere. A person who commands the transdisciplines learns a new domain in weeks, not years.

### 3. A "Bucket of Skills" Is Not an Integrated Capability

A team where each member can do A, B, and C separately is not the same as a team that has mastered the integrated workflow A+B+C. The emergent value of integration is invisible when you look only at skills.

Mastery = skills + coordination + judgment + method selection. The last three are not skills — they are knowledge.

### 4. Skills Do Not Generate New Skills

Principles are generative: new methods for new situations can be derived from them. Skills are executive: they work only in situations they were trained on.

When a technological shift occurs — AI replaces functions, new professions emerge — an agent with principles creates new roles. An agent with only skills searches for retraining courses.

## The Generative Chain

```
ZP (think)  →  FPF (describe)  →  Pack (know)  →  DS (do)
     ↓               ↓                  ↓               ↓
 Transfer        Correctness        Domain language   Skills
 across          of descriptions    and methods       and programs
 domains
```

Each level **generates** the next. Without the upper levels, the lower levels do not emerge — or they emerge slowly and remain fragile.

## What This Means for the IWE User

IWE is built on this hierarchy:

| IWE Component | Principles Level |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Methodology (FPF/SPF, protocols) | Level 0–1 |
| Exocortex slot (CLAUDE.md, memory/) | Level 1–2 |
| AI roles (Strategist, Extractor) | Level 2–3 |
| Workspace (DS-strategy/) | Level 3 |
| Tools (Git, MCP, bot) | Level 3 |

An IWE user does not simply receive tools (Level 3). They receive a **methodology** (Level 0–1) and **domain knowledge** (Level 2) that allow them to generate new skills and adapt to change.

## Connection to Development Formats (MIM)

| Level | What is learned | Format |
|-------|----------------|--------|
| ZP/FPF | Principles | Seminar (introduction) → Workshop (automation) |
| Pack | Domain knowledge | Seminar + Workshop |
| DS | Skills | Workshop → Residency (work result) |
